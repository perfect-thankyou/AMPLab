June 2026

A note from this month's vlog

The brain research Erianna mentioned: a growing body of peer-reviewed studies has shown that becoming a parent — through pregnancy, but also through fatherhood and adoption — physically restructures the brain. The changes are concentrated in regions involved in empathy, reading emotions, and bonding with a child. They scale with hands-on caregiving: the more involved the parent, the more pronounced the changes. If you've ever felt like your brain is doing too much, it kind of is — and it's doing it for you and your family.

Some of the studies, if you're curious:

  • Dopamine drives persistent remodeling of the maternal brain (Nature, 2026)
  • Pregnancy entails a U-shaped trajectory in human brain structure (Nature Communications, 2025)
  • First-time fathers show longitudinal gray matter cortical volume reductions (Cerebral Cortex, 2023)
  • The paternal brain: longitudinal insights into structural and functional plasticity (Translational Psychiatry, 2026)
